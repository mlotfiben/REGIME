# REGIME — XAUUSD Directional Regime Filter: Complete Experiment Recap

**Date:** 2026-08-23 · **Repo:** `mlotfiben/REGIME` (all results committed & pushed)
**Purpose:** This document is written to be read by an external reviewer (e.g. Claude) with
no prior context. It summarizes every experiment, its output, and the conclusions, with the
honest numbers and the caveats. Source files are cited so any number can be verified.

---

## 0. The question and why it matters

The owner has spent years trying to predict the **direction** of gold (XAUUSD) from price
history alone. The program's standing finding was **"direction is dead"** — every previous
directional test (news sentiment, candle escalation, inflections, DL) came back null or
sub-operational, while **magnitude/volatility** was the one axis that showed real, transferable
signal. A respected external critique (the "regime filter" proposal) argued the right framing is
**not** "predict the next bar's sign" but a **sparse, conditional** edge: detect a trend/chop
regime and only trade the strong cases.

This project (REGIME) tested that hypothesis rigorously. It found a real directional signal —
then showed, economically, that it is not exploitable on OHLC alone. Both halves of that
conclusion are the deliverables.

**Asset/timeframe:** XAUUSD, raw 1-minute (6.82M bars, 2004–2026) resampled to
1m / 15m / 30m / 1h / 4h. Target frequency for the headline tests: **1h.**

---

## 1. Method (all pre-registered, library-first)

- **Target A (frozen):** does price hit **+2·ATR before −1·ATR** within the next H bars?
  (López de Prado triple-barrier style, minus the explicit time barrier). Base rate ~0.10 at 1h.
- **Features (causal, all data ≤ t, no lookahead):** multi-horizon momentum (1h/5h/1d),
  trend-efficiency (net move / total path), channel breakout, Kalman slope (`filterpy`, causal
  filter-only — no smoother), volatility-regime gate. Later enhanced with **fractional
  differentiation** (AFML Ch.5, implemented in-house — no maintained PyPI lib exists) and a
  **London/NY session** feature.
- **Model:** transparent logistic regression, walk-forward (train strictly before test,
  purged + embargoed). No deep model — one asset, OHLC only.
- **Discipline:** mandatory A–D gate (synthetic positive/negative controls) before any real
  data; day-clustered / month-block bootstrap statistics (never naive per-bar); deflation for
  the ~20+ prior trials; fair baseline = **risk-matched constant exposure** (not a strawman);
  no-lookahead unit tests.
- **Library-first rule:** Kalman from `filterpy`, statistics from scipy/sklearn, the causal Re_t
  gauge imported from the canonical COLORSLOPE module. Only `fracdiff` was written in-house
  (documented, unit-tested) because no maintained library exists.

## 2. Experiment 1 — Gate D: does the pipeline work at all? (synthetic controls)

**Result: PASS.** Before touching real data, the pipeline was validated on synthetic series:

| control | AUC | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| positive (injected drift-switching regime) | **0.861** | pipeline recovers a known edge |
| negative (same structure, no drift) | **0.497** | pipeline stays silent when effect absent |

The machinery is correctly wired. Source: `references/gateR1_result.md`, `engine/gate_d.py`.

---

## 3. Experiment 2 — Gate R1 at 1m: is there ANY signal?

**Result: real-but-tiny, decaying to operationally null.** Walk-forward AUC on the full 1m
series, honest month-block bootstrap:

| fold | overall AUC | month-block 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (oldest) | 0.5331 | [0.5285, 0.5375] |
| 2 | 0.5219 | — |
| 3 | 0.5156 | — |
| 4 (most recent) | 0.5070 | [0.5023, 0.5109] |

The naive day-clustered t=44.5 / p≈0 was **inflated** by 1m label autocorrelation (H=60 overlap
within and across days); the month-block CI is the honest width. Even the "significant" recent
fold is only significant because n is enormous — AUC 0.507 is operationally meaningless.

**Conclusion:** the method works in the narrow statistical sense but 1m is too noisy /
momentum-dominated. Source: `references/gateR1_result.md`.

## 4. Experiment 3 — AUC vs candle timeframe (the ray of light)

**Result: a clear non-monotonic peak at 1h.** Walk-forward AUC, wall-clock-consistent windows,
month-block bootstrap CI. This is the strongest positive finding.

| timeframe | bars | AUC (full regime set) | month-block CI | momentum-only | base rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1m | 6.82M | 0.519 | [0.516, 0.522] | 0.518 | 0.374 |
| 15m | 496k | 0.589 | [0.584, 0.595] | 0.496 | 0.277 |
| 30m | 249k | 0.620 | [0.614, 0.627] | 0.509 | 0.190 |
| **1h** | 126k | **0.636** | [0.627, 0.644] | 0.519 | 0.105 |
| 4h | 33k | 0.589 | [0.567, 0.612] | 0.551 | 0.023 |

Two key facts:
1. **Every timeframe ≥15m beats momentum-only by a wide margin** (0.59–0.64 vs 0.50–0.55).
   The *regime combination* carries signal beyond plain momentum (which the program already knew
   was weak). This is the "source" the owner sought.
2. **Toy-model controls confirmed the pipeline:** pure-sine → AUC **1.000** (perfectly recovers
   clean periodic structure), trend/chop drift-switching → **0.693**. The machinery finds real
   structure when it exists.

Source: `references/gateR1_result.md` (sweep), `engine/auc_vs_timeframe.py`,
`results/auc_vs_timeframe.png`, `results/auc_vs_timeframe.json`.

## 5. Experiment 4 — Decision-level test: does the edge survive costs?

**The decisive test.** An accuracy AUC is not an economic verdict. The strategy: long-only,
enter when p ≥ 80th-pctile (causal), exit at +2ATR / −1ATR / time, one round-trip cost per
trade. **Fair baseline = risk-matched constant exposure** (same average exposure held flat).

**Result: FAIL at 1h and 30m.** Cost-sensitivity curve at 1h (const Sharpe = 3.56):

| cost | scheme Sharpe | const Sharpe |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 8.26 | 3.56 |
| 0.01% | 4.75 | 3.56 |
| **0.02%** | **1.22** | 3.56 |
| 0.05% | −9.34 | 3.56 |
| 0.10% | −25.6 | 3.56 |

Bootstrap P(scheme worse than its own constant exposure) @ 0.02% = **0.970 (1h) / 0.9985 (30m)**.
Gross Sharpe ~8 is an illusion — 5,600+ round-trips over 20 years at ~0.2 exposure means the
thin barrier edge cannot pay the turnover.

Source: `references/gateR1_decision_result.md`, `engine/decision_backtest.py`.

## 6. Experiment 5 — Two literature-backed attempts to fix turnover (parallel)

Two ways to cut turnover, run in parallel, same discipline:

- **Variant R (Re_t/vol-regime gate):** reuse the owner's existing causal Re_t gauge as a
  trade gate (only trade the laminar/high-conviction half).
- **Variant M (meta-labeling, AFML Ch.3.6/50):** a secondary logistic predicts "will this
  primary trade be profitable" and only top-confidence trades are taken.

**Result (corrected magnitudes):**

| variant | n_trades (20y) | Sharpe @ 0.02% | P(scheme worse) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base (no filter) | 5,659 | 1.22 | 0.970 |
| R (Re_t gate) | 2,253 | −0.85 | 0.9993 |
| **M (meta-labeling)** | **326** | **1.62** | **0.9273** |

- **Re_t as a gate FAILED** (Sharpe −0.85) — it removes good and bad trades alike, and
  independently re-confirms the program's Gate C1 (instability-gating on gold doesn't work).
- **Meta-labeling is the best variant** (fewest trades, highest net Sharpe) but **still loses to
  the risk-matched constant** (P 0.9273).

Source: `references/gateR1_variants_result.md`, `engine/decision_variants.py`.

## 7. Experiment 6 — Edge-strengthening: raise the AUC (fracdiff + session)

Owner asked: can we add +3–5% AUC? Literature-backed feature engineering:
fractional differentiation (AFML Ch.5) + London/NY session.

**Result: AUC rose but economics got WORSE.**

| feature set | mean AUC | month-block CI |
|---|---|---|
| BASE (7 features) | 0.6388 | [0.6271, 0.6444] |
| **ENHANCED (10: +fracdiff, session)** | **0.6678** | [0.6554, 0.6737] |

AUC gain **+0.029**, CIs non-overlapping → real. **BUT** the meta-labeling decision test on
enhanced features got *worse* (Sharpe 1.75, P 0.903). Mechanism: richer features made the
meta-label **over-confident** → selected only 350 ultra-confident trades whose per-trade edge
still doesn't beat flat.

**Central finding: AUC is NOT the binding constraint. The economics are.** A +3-5% AUC is
achievable and economically irrelevant here. Source: `references/gateR1_edge_strengthening.md`.

## 8. Experiment 7 — Precision vs Recall: the final lens (and a critical bug catch)

Owner asked: controlling precision vs recall, at 20% recall what precision, and where does cost
win? This analysis **caught a critical bug**.

**⚠ Integrity fix:** the trade simulators let a trade **re-enter immediately at its exit bar**
when consecutive bars passed the threshold → overlapping/adjacent positions inflated both trade
count and returns. **This had contaminated the earlier decision-test magnitudes** (they showed
gross Sharpe ~10 and better net numbers). After fixing (no immediate re-entry, one position at a
time), every earlier verdict **was re-run and stood**, with honest (lower) magnitudes.

**Honest PR frontier (1h enhanced, 0.02% cost):**

| recall | precision | n_trades | scheme Sharpe | diff vs const |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.662 | 0.480 | 12,965 | 2.08 | −1.48 |
| 0.586 | 0.478 | 11,526 | 2.54 | **−1.02 (best)** |
| 0.212 | 0.449 | 4,441 | −0.48 | −4.04 |
| **0.106 (~20%)** | 0.450 | 2,208 | −0.60 | **−4.16** |

**Answers to the owner's questions:**
- **Precision at ~20% recall ≈ 0.45** — but scheme Sharpe −0.67 vs const 3.56 → far worse than flat.
- **Precision is essentially flat (~0.45–0.48) across the ENTIRE recall range** — the model
  cannot trade precision against recall usefully.
- **There is no recall level where the scheme beats holding gold flat.** Best diff anywhere is −1.02.

Source: `references/gateR1_precision_recall.md`, `engine/precision_recall.py`,
`results/precision_recall_econ.png`.

## 10. The honest overall conclusion

1. **A real, conditional, directional signal on gold OHLC exists** — it was found (AUC ~0.64 at
   1h, robustly above momentum-only, with a working pipeline proven by toy controls). This is a
   genuine, novel positive result for a program that had declared direction dead.
2. **It is not economically exploitable** on OHLC alone. Every attempt to make it survive costs —
   fewer trades (meta-labeling), regime gating (Re_t), more precision (PR), stronger features
   (fracdiff) — fails against the fair baseline (risk-matched constant exposure). The per-trade
   edge-to-cost ratio is simply too thin.
3. **AUC is not the binding constraint; turnover×cost is.** This is the transferable lesson: a
   "+3-5% AUC" is achievable and economically meaningless here.
4. **The bug find matters:** the overlap bug inflated earlier economic magnitudes. The verdict
   was robust to it (FAIL either way), but the corrected magnitudes are the honest ones.
5. **No live test was run** — and that is correct. There is nothing economically validated to
   take live.

**Classification (evidence hierarchy):** the directional signal is **predictive but
operationally irrelevant** on OHLC alone. Filed, not shipped, not discarded.

## 11. Scoping (overclaim rule)

These conclusions are scoped to: XAUUSD OHLC, Target A (+2ATR/−1ATR barrier), these 10 causal
features, logistic walk-forward, long-only, cost 0–0.10% round-trip. They do NOT claim:
- no directional signal exists anywhere (only that it's not economically exploitable on OHLC at
  these timeframes with this method);
- that a different model family, a genuinely different cost regime, a different barrier
  definition, or **a magnitude/volatility target** (the program's actual validated edge domain,
  not tested here) could not change the result.

## 12. What I'd ask a reviewer (open questions / honest doubts)

1. **Is the 1h AUC 0.636 credible, or could it be an artifact?** It contradicts the program's
   "direction is dead" null, so I want an adversarial check on the feature alignment / label
   causality (the no-lookahead unit test passes, but a fresh pair of eyes on the barrier-label
   construction would help).
2. **Is the risk-matched-constant the right economic bar, or is it too strict for a genuinely
   sparse edge?** It's the methodology's standard, but for a low-base-rate target it may be a
   very high bar.
3. **Where would YOU look for the source of the ray?** The signal is real but thin. Is there a
   defensible path to close the edge-to-cost gap (e.g. magnitude/vol target, a genuinely lower-cost
   execution, a different entry that isn't turnover-heavy), or is OHLC-only direction a dead end?
4. **Is the "AUC ≠ economics" finding (fracdiff raised AUC but hurt decision test) a known
   phenomenon with a cleaner explanation** than my "meta-label over-confidence" read?
